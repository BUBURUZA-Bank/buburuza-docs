$$BUB is the native token of Buburuza-Chain, and it does a lot more than most utility tokens. You use it to pay gas fees on every on-chain transaction, access premium AI features, participate in protocol governance, and receive discounts on trading and banking services. At the same time, $$BUB is designed to become scarcer over time: 60% of every base transaction fee is permanently burned, and additional burns come from AI service usage, bridge operations, and treasury buybacks. This page covers what \$BUB is, how it's distributed, and how the DAO that governs it works.

## Token overview

$$BUB is a hybrid utility and governance token. Unlike purely inflationary Proof-of-Stake tokens, $$BUB has no automatic new issuance beyond its initial distribution schedule. The only mechanism that creates additional \$BUB after launch is the sequencer rewards pool — and that pool is drawn from the fixed initial supply, not minted on demand.

## Deflationary mechanisms

Four distinct processes permanently reduce the \$BUB supply over time. Unlike buyback programs that recycle tokens, these burns are irreversible — the tokens are sent to `0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dead` and removed from circulation forever.

### 1. Transaction fee burns (60% of base fees)

Every time anyone transacts on Buburuza-Chain, 60% of the base fee is permanently burned. This mechanism scales directly with network activity — the more the network is used, the faster the supply shrinks.

### 2. AI service burns (100% of fees)

When you use premium AI features — document processing, KYC/AML verification, advanced analytics, or compliance reporting — the entire fee is burned. No portion goes to the treasury or sequencers.

### 3. Cross-chain bridge burns (50–75% of bridge fees)

Every asset transfer across chains includes a burn component:

- Bridge transaction fees: 50% burned, 50% to operators.
- Interoperability protocol costs: 75% burned, 25% to treasury.

### 4. Treasury buybacks

During periods of low network activity, the treasury executes automated buyback-and-burn programs. These serve as a floor mechanism — maintaining supply pressure even when organic transaction volume is lower.

## Governance framework

$$BUB is the governance token for Buburuza-Chain's DAO. Holding $$BUB gives you a say in how the protocol evolves — from fee parameters to treasury spending to major upgrades.

### Voting power

Your voting power is not simply proportional to how many tokens you hold. The DAO uses a formula designed to reward long-term commitment and prevent any single large holder from dominating decisions:

```text
Voting Power = sqrt(Staked $BUB Amount) × Time Lock Multiplier × Delegation Factor
```

The square root function means a holder with 1,000,000 \$BUB has 1,000 units of base voting power — not 1,000,000. This reduces plutocracy while still rewarding larger stakeholders.

### Time lock multipliers

Locking your \$BUB for longer periods increases your voting power. This aligns long-term holders with the protocol's long-term health.

| Lock period | Voting power multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| No lock | 1.0× |
| 3 months | 1.2× |
| 6 months | 1.5× |
| 1 year | 2.0× |
| 2 years | 2.5× |

### Delegation

If you hold \$BUB but don't want to vote on every proposal yourself, you can delegate your voting power to any address you trust. Delegates build on-chain reputation scores based on their participation history and proposal outcomes.

## Sequencer economics

Running a sequencer on Buburuza-Chain requires posting a bond and maintaining strict performance standards. In return, sequencers earn 35% of all network transaction fees.

### Bonding requirements

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum bond | 100,000 \$BUB per sequencer |
| Maximum initial operators | 5–10 (expandable via governance) |
| Bond reviews | Quarterly, based on network growth |
| Slashing range | 5–50% of bond for provable misconduct |

### Performance standards

Sequencer rewards are not split equally — they're distributed proportionally to a performance score.

| Component | Weight | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uptime | 40% | 99.9% availability |
| Latency | 30% | Sub-250ms block production |
| Throughput | 20% | Transaction processing efficiency |
| Community score | 10% | Governance participation and contributions |

### Slashing and penalties

| Infraction type | Slash amount | Example violations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor infractions | 5% of bond | Late blocks, brief downtime |
| Major violations | 20% of bond | Extended downtime, failed upgrades |
| Critical failures | 50% of bond | Consensus attacks, data withholding |

Slashed funds are distributed as follows: 50% burned permanently, 30% distributed to other operators as bonus rewards, and 20% allocated to the treasury for network security improvements.

<Tip>
  Sequencer slashing creates an additional deflationary mechanism — the 50% of slashed funds that are burned reduce supply further even if it's a rare event.
</Tip>
